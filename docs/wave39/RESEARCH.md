# Primary-source design hypotheses

Thomas Grip explains that inconsistent consequences break a player's ability to plan. Applied inference: real manual departure plus stale autopilot help is confusing; letting the crew cancel gives a rescue/return choice with visible consequence. [Frictional Games, Choices, Consequences and the Ability to Plan](https://frictionalgames.com/2017-06-choices-consequences-and-the-ability-to-plan/).

David Pittman's Eldritch talk describes using dead-end loot to give side space a purpose and landmarks to distinguish generated areas. Applied inference: move one already-rolled small valuable to an accessible optional bay branch, preserving ordinary quick cargo and all reward rolls. This is not a claim that every seed has a suitable branch or that the change increases retention. [GDC2015, Procedural Level Design in Eldritch](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2015/presentations/Pittman_David_Procedural%20Level%20Design.pdf).

Valve's playtesting talk treats design as a hypothesis tested through observation. The relevant questions here are: what is the next action, what caused a decision to turn back, did the crew understand why it failed, and did it voluntarily begin another shift? Guided native input verifies the transport/custody/economy path, not those human conclusions. [Valve's Approach to Playtesting](https://cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/GDC2009_ValvesApproachToPlaytesting.pdf).

Candidate deferred: peak-fade recovery pacing, informed by [Mike Booth, The AI Systems of Left4Dead](https://cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf). TFG already has a calm/build/peak/relax director and threat density controls. No observed shift currently justifies adding another director or changing its recovery timer.

Human follow-up after a blind shift: Where did you stop knowing what to do? Which risk did you deliberately take? Was the failure preventable from the cues? What would you do differently next shift? Record confusion, first meaningful choice and voluntary retry, not an invented numerical fun score.
