# Wave27 — principles behind the salvage expansion

This round reread the official store texts downloaded and reviewed in Wave25;
these are cached primary-source descriptions, not a fresh October market survey
or proof that we played those games. See [Wave25 research](../wave25/RESEARCH.md)
for its retrieval method and broader horror/playtest/engineering sources.

| Source | Useful design promise | Original TFG application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [R.E.P.O.](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3241660/REPO/) | Physical valuable objects, fragile versus heavy haul, teamwork and knowing when to be quiet. | Existing grab/cart/carry foundations gain real opposing cargo braking and a rattling Reply Drum. No franchise monsters, commercial meshes or copied room layouts. |
| [Deep Rock Galactic](https://store.steampowered.com/app/548430/Deep_Rock_Galactic/) | Complementary crew toolkits and bringing the crew home. | Useful spotter/operator/carrier roles arise from current movement/E/cargo tools without mandatory classes or another unlock tree. |
| [Content Warning](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2881650/Content_Warning/) | Recovering content after a dangerous expedition creates a shared crew story. | The Algorithm's dead-link archive, physical receipt/recovery props and noisy cargo express the existing dead-internet identity. Existing film/feed systems are not reimplemented. |
| [Valve, Left4Dead AI](https://cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf) | Pressure and recovery alternate; pacing differs from difficulty. | A noisy release uses the native hearing stream rather than spawning a punishment wave. Ordinary optional retrieval leaves quiet exploration and direct lift return available. |

The content question is concrete: does the crew choose who handles the cargo,
when to brake, whether to spend four quiet seconds or make one quick noise, and
whether to leave a rattling valuable to escape? More item names alone cannot
answer this. Counts, bounded queues and low draw batches establish implementation
costs, not fun, retention or hardware FPS. Independent human full shifts remain
needed to measure whether these decisions are enjoyable.
