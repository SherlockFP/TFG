# Maintenance lift readability

The existing maintenance cabin receives an original archive wayfinding layer: narrow dirty-ivory doorway facings, restrained ochre threshold paint and three numbered back-panel icons. The bell marks CALL, the descending arrow marks DOWN, and the upward arrow with a surface line marks SURFACE. Labels and short status lines support English, Turkish and Russian. Small ground arrowheads point outward through the actual cabin mouth; they indicate leaving the cabin, not a promised route to a distant surface exit.

The geometry follows the existing lift-local orientation and stays at its doorway/console surfaces. Console anchors, standing clearance, physics, navigation and gameplay stay with the native lift builder. The panels are readable without adding a dynamic light: small non-emissive Basic-material signage contrasts with the matte steel cabin. There is no new mandatory HUD, green portal, floating screen or gameplay system.

`buildDescent23Art({root,plan,anchors,floor})` returns `setState(next)`, `dispose()` and `metrics`. The layer adds **4 batches / 286 triangles**, **zero lights and colliders**, and one nearest-filtered **768 × 192** atlas. One canvas is retained for the lifetime of the cabin. Unchanged canonical state does not repaint/upload the atlas; changed state or language does. Each button shows its own condition: discovered CALL reads READY TO CALL; DOWN asks CALL FIRST until the native ready stage; a deep SURFACE reads DIRECT RETURN even before discovery. Surface-level return stays dim. Busy transit overrides all captions. Native E prompts remain authoritative.

The helper exclusively owns its geometry, four materials, atlas and language listener. The native builder integrates it after its original ownership census and successful physical placement validation, forwards canonical state changes (including `ready`, derived from the native ready lift stage), and disposes the helper before removing its root. Marked `shared`/`keep` resources prevent generic facility cleanup from disposing them a second time; helper disposal is idempotent.

An actual Three.js headless smoke check passed localized labels, unchanged-state redraw suppression, bounded geometry/resources and exactly one disposal event for each of nine owned resources. Native builder tests cover the integrated Rapier routes and resource cleanup separately. Eye-height readability and actual E selection remain browser QA evidence; geometry counts alone do not prove either or hardware frame rate.
