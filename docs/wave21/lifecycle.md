# Depth transit lifecycle

Ordinary expeditions can continue through streamed procedural floors. Surface depth zero keeps the actual initial seed, theme, size and generation options. Explore up to fifteen reachable ordinary rooms to authorize that floor's physical lift. The whole living crew boards before descent; downed crew must first be recovered. Calling and travelling each take three seconds of host simulation. Leaving the cabin cancels travel safely. A deep lift always permits a shared express return, even before that floor's discovery target is met.

Only one indoor facility exists at a time. The outdoor map, ship, native wallets/quota/day, held and bag inventory remain. Loose cargo and corpses inside the cabin retain native item IDs during transit. Loose owned cargo outside the cabin blocks transit rather than being discarded or remotely pulled aboard. Cargo is limited to 64 serialized native objects; the one surface checkpoint holds at most 256 loose objects. Overflow cancels before changing anything.

The surface checkpoint refreshes from actual remaining loose items and doors each time the crew leaves depth zero. Returning regenerates the same original floor and restores those exact IDs, not fresh loot. This refresh prevents formerly picked-up surface loot from duplicating on a later return. Base darkness-collapse state and native spent indoor spawn budget also return. Deep abandoned floors are never archived or revisited: after depths one and two, returning to the surface leads next to depth three.

Each new deep floor receives bounded native loot once. Archive rules favor deeper room spots; heavy rules replace at most two ordinary entries with native big salvage, preserving the same total count. Native threat budgets and damage caps belong to the separate descent-threat installer. Only indoor creatures are silently removed on a swap, without kill XP. New deep indoor budget resets; outdoor budget/day statistics stay unchanged. The native extraction clock is held in deep mode and travel, resuming on the surface; the policy is explicit in the physical prompt.

## Authority, generation and lifecycle

`installDescent21(game)` exposes `spec(run)`, `onState()`, `state()`, `hostReq()`, `plan()`, `commit()`, `placeLateJoin()`, `acceptItemEvent()`, `controlsActive()`, `clockRate()` and `dispose()`. `run.descent21` holds one bounded checkpoint, current depth/reached depth, current generation choice, room IDs, revision/nonce and lift stage. Stage duration uses replicated elapsed simulation, published every quarter second. It cannot inherit a wildly different successor host clock or instantly catch up a stalled frame.

Requests validate the native host, landing token, revision, nonce, living indoor caller, reach and physical line of sight. Descent/return also validate every living crew member in the cabin before warning and again before commitment; active pursuit/mission/instance travel is blocked. Special HQ/home/custom/expedition/goal/instance/core/raid/voyage/ghost maps are excluded.

An isolated real Rapier preflight certifies each target lobby before removing the old facility. Live rebuilding uses the builder's verified native-layout certificate and never advances the live physics world outside its fixed step. The primary deterministic floor has two bounded fallback choices (factory, then Thread Archive); the actual seed/theme/size is saved and replicated for late join. These are safety retries before any loot exists, not rerollable floors or extra rewards. If all choices fail, the existing floor remains intact.

The host-only `ds21floor` packet carries the floor state, certificate, crew and serialized cabin cargo before native loot spawns. Packet processing has its own receipt, so a normal run snapshot arriving first does not lose cargo relocation or crew transport; newer same-floor room progress is preserved. Duplicate packets cannot rebuild/repopulate a floor. Abandoned loose indoor objects receive native reliable `it.rm` messages after prior spawns; held/bag, outdoor and cabin IDs are excluded. Native network and welcome item handlers reject a deep-stage loose spawn whose ID belongs to the bounded surface checkpoint. This prevents a delayed surface restoration packet recreating sealed loot after another descent, without a second item manager or an unbounded retired-ID ledger. Root's normal welcome/save loader uses `spec()` and calls `onState()` before item snapshots. A new late join is placed aboard after the next ordinary physics step.

`facilityWillChange(world,game,targetDepth)` fires before lift/old-facility disposal. `facilityChanged(world,game,depth)` fires after assigning the new facility and depth. General `mapLoaded` never fires during swaps, preventing outdoor quests or normal moon population from duplicating. Deep native exits direct crew back to the lift rather than instant outdoor travel.

## Evidence and limits

`descent21_lifecycle.test.mjs` runs the actual module with real Rapier facility geometry and native ItemManager events/serialization through `0 → 1 → 2 → 0 → 3`. It covers held/bag/corpse/outdoor custody, finite IDs and loot count, no surface pickup duplication, cohort rejection, nonce validation, indoor/outdoor budget separation, both snapshot/packet orderings, delayed surface restoration rejection, ordered abandoned deep-item removal and valid custody snapshots, duplicate packets, late join, lifecycle cleanup and call/travel migration with host clock offsets of 10,000/20,000 seconds. Builder and threat tests separately cover capsule clearance, certificates and pre-broadcast limits. Browser feel, Internet packet transport and human long-session retention remain QA work.

The native interaction selector suppresses only explicitly marked `optionalPeer` invitations while physically inside the active lift cabin. RPS, high-five and player trade share that policy; chat/keyboard commands, NPC commerce, revive, doors and body/heavy cargo retain their native paths. `descent21_focus.test.mjs` installs all three invitation producers and exercises actual native target selection, including mandatory rescue/cargo/door alternatives.
